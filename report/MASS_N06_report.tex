\begin{abstract}
Survey questions often reuse answer scales, even when they ask about different subjects. We use this structure to predict how a respondent would answer each question given their observed background. Our entry, N06, fits a small transformer separately on the visible respondents in each survey. Each answer has its own prediction weights and a second term shared across answers through embeddings of the published questionnaire wording. We compute the embeddings once with a frozen MiniLM encoder. The model assigns probabilities to all allowed outcomes, including a distinct outcome for questions skipped by design. On the development split, N06 scored 0.44617 overall. Correctly assigning the wording embeddings improved the released World Bank score relative to a within-question shuffle, while the UNICEF and UNHCR scores tied. Official test-phase Grand Prize score: \TestGrand.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Pipeline}
Before evaluation, we encode each legal answer on its own and alongside the question it answers. During evaluation, a transformer maps the respondent's always-visible fields to a single vector. Each question has a classifier that uses this vector to predict the respondent's answers. The wording embeddings connect the classifiers, allowing a response to one question to affect what the model learns about another. Each survey has its own fitted model. Only the fixed wording features share a coordinate system across surveys.

\subsection{Inputs and feature construction}
We use every field labelled \code{GIVEN} in the public schemas \citep{bench}. Examples include religious affiliation and rural or urban residence for UNICEF, the randomised feedback arm and reservation pay for the World Bank, and survey wave and country of asylum for UNHCR. Each field contributes a token with learned embeddings for its identity and its categorical value. The encoder omits identifiers and excluded fields. Answers to \code{PREDICT} questions supply training labels only. We use neither survey weights nor external respondent records.

For each option we embed the answer label and the label paired with the question. We subtract the mean embedding across options of that question in each channel. Two projections fitted to the public dictionaries compress those differences to 64 features per answer (Appendix~\ref{app:config}). For gated questions we append an option describing a routing skip. For example, UNICEF asks about willingness to get vaccinated after a respondent reports no prior vaccination. The model assigns separate probabilities to willingness, unwillingness, uncertainty and having skipped that question.

\subsection{Model or simulator}
Two transformer encoder layers with four attention heads produce a 64-dimensional summary $h_i$ of respondent $i$ \citep{attention}. Let $v_{jk}$ be the fixed wording vector for answer $k$ to question $j$. With option-specific weight $a_{jk}$, bias $b_{jk}$ and survey-wide matrix $W$, the logit is
\begin{equation}
 z_{ijk}=h_i^\top a_{jk}+b_{jk}+\tfrac12 h_i^\top Wv_{jk}.
 \label{eq:readout}
\end{equation}
The first two terms let identically worded options behave differently across questions. The last term lets a training example update scores for other options with similar vectors. We initialise $W$ to zero, so the contribution from wording is learned from visible responses. If any option key fails to match the runtime schema, that question uses its independent weights and bias alone.

\subsection{Fitting, training, or prompting}
Within each survey, we minimise mean categorical cross-entropy over visible cells of items with at least two observed response categories. Each eligible cell has equal weight. We train with AdamW in batches of 256. An inner holdout of whole respondents selects the epoch count using mean held-out log probability after smoothing and the grader's probability floor. We then reinitialise the network and train on all fitting respondents for that many epochs. The wrapper also runs a preliminary fit on a capped subset. The standalone entry always uses the final all-visible fit for prediction. Appendix~\ref{app:config} gives the split sizes and stopping rule.

\subsection{Prediction and aggregation}
A separate softmax over the declared answers gives one probability vector per blank cell in schema order. For gated questions the model learns the probability of a recorded skip directly from the background fields, including when a routing parent's answer is hidden. The grader scores the probabilities assigned to the realised answers, then averages the three survey skills with equal weight \citep{grader}.

\subsection{Post-processing and calibration (if applicable)}
For an item with $K$ answers, let $n_k$ be the visible count for option $k$. We mix the softmax distribution with the smoothed item marginal $m_k=(n_k+0.5)/(\sum_\ell n_\ell+0.5K)$, using weights 0.98 and 0.02 respectively. An item with fewer than two observed categories uses $m$ alone. This gives positive probability to legal answers absent from training. If the fitting time guard is reached, the wrapper returns the visible-data marginals for that survey.

\section{Limitations}
Identical background fields yield identical distributions, even for people with different unobserved experiences. N06 predicts each question separately and can give positive probability to incompatible answer combinations if sampled as a complete questionnaire. Aggregate log scores measure predictive performance over scored cells and cannot establish calibration for an individual question, country or demographic group.

\subsection{Wording features}
For each scored item, enumerate its legal options and append \code{NA_GATED} if the schema defines a gate. Render ordinary options verbatim, including numeric codes. Render the skip as \texttt{Not asked because of survey routing}. The two encoder inputs are the label and \texttt{Answer: <label>. Question: <question>}. Encode unique strings on CPU in batches of 32, mean-pool nonpadding token states, and L2-normalise. The longest paired input uses 66 of the 256 available tokens.

For each channel, centre the 384-dimensional vectors within question. Fit a singular value decomposition to the centred rows of all three dictionaries and retain the leading 32 directions. Orient each direction so its largest absolute loading is positive. Within each question divide by the root mean squared projected option norm, lower-bounded by $10^{-6}$. Concatenate the channels, scale by $1/\sqrt{2}$ and store the coordinates as float16. The table has 1,550 rows: 61 UNICEF, 655 World Bank and 834 UNHCR. At runtime the code checks the asset digest, looks up each option by a hash of its field name, question, value and gate status, and centres each item's matched float32 vectors before applying Eq.~\ref{eq:readout}.
